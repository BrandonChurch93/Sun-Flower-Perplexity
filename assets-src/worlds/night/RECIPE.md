# Recipe · world night

## glow.jpg

- Output files: `public/assets/worlds/night/full/glow.jpg` (3660 x 2059, JPEG quality 93, no chroma subsampling) and `public/assets/worlds/night/web/glow.jpg` (1830 x 1030, JPEG quality 92, no chroma subsampling).
- What it is: the soft light of the night sky only: the glowing clouds of the Milky Way with their dark lanes, the deep blue black above and the pale teal glow low on the left. It holds no stars, no land, no plant and no moon. The stars are points drawn by code in `src/worlds/night/sky.ts`.
- Where it lies: on the plane of the camera of the key `night` (bearing -167.24 degrees, 9.53 degrees up, 47.4 degrees wide), reaching 1.3 times as far as that frame in every direction. It is fixed to the stars and turns with them about the pole.
- Tool: the workspace's image generation tool, model `seedream_5`, used as an image edit, then `node assets-src/worlds/night/compose-glow.mjs`, run from the repo root.
- Steps:
  1. The take. Input: `references/approved/night.png`, as the only input image. Aspect ratio 16:9. Prompt, word for word: "Edit this photograph. Remove the tall sunflower plant in the foreground completely: its stem, all of its leaves and its closed bud. Fill the place where it stood with what would be seen behind it: the night sky full of stars with the Milky Way, the dark hills and the dark field below them. Keep everything else exactly as it is and exactly where it is: the framing, the horizon line, the sun and its position, the sky, the colors, the light, the depth of field blur and the film grain. Do not add anything: no plant in the foreground, no sprout, no flower, no leaves, no people, no animals, no insects, no objects, no text, no watermark, no border." The take came back at 2816 x 1584. It is read from `out/work/sky-night/take-plant-out.jpg`, which is not committed.
  2. `compose-glow.mjs` takes the stars out of the take at its own size, 2816 x 1584, with a median: per channel the middle value of the 7 x 7 pixels around each pixel, so a star, which fills less than half of that window, is gone and the dark lanes and the knots of the clouds keep their edges and their depth. A second median of 25 x 25 pixels is used only where the first is still more than 60 percent brighter than it (linear luminance, the mark blurred by 5 pixels), which removes the few wide star discs that are left. Then a blur of 1 pixel, never brighter than the take itself. What is left is the light that is wider than a star. The two windows and the 60 percent are the arguments 4, 5 and 6 of the script.
  3. It finds the top of the land in every column (the steepest fall of brightness between 0.655 and 0.81 of the height), and from 14 pixels above it downward it lays the tone of the sky just above the land, eased over 96 columns either way, so the sky goes on behind the hills that the code draws.
  4. It adds a border of 0.15 of the frame on every side by mirroring the take across its own edge, so the clouds of the Milky Way go on past the frame with their own grain and no column of one tone stands in it. From the edge outward the border is eased over 240 pixels into a copy of itself blurred by 18 pixels. Then it writes both files, the full one at the size of the take with its border and the web one at half of that.
  5. It writes `src/worlds/night/density.ts`: the brightness of the map on a grid of 256 x 144, which the star catalogue uses so that the stars stand denser along the Milky Way and thin out in its dark lanes. The grid is fine enough that a lane 15 pixels wide in the frame stays empty of code stars.
- Take kept: take 1 of 2: the plant is gone and the Milky Way, the hills and the mist sit exactly where they do in the approved image (best shift 0 px in the left, right and top strips, searched within 20 px). Take thrown out for this map: a second edit that asked the model to remove the land and every star itself (`out/work/sky-night/glow-1.png`, same model): it kept many stars in the core, darkened the corners and moved the low glow from the left edge to the middle.
- Checks passed: the composed map was looked at whole and beside the approved image at the core: no star left as a point or a blob over the part the film sees, no blue plateau at the core, no land, no plant, no seam where the sky goes on below the hill line, no text, no watermark. Sizes: 3660 x 2059 and 1830 x 1030. Files: 0.52 MB and 0.17 MB. In memory with its smaller copies the full map takes 40.2 MB.
- How the film shows it: `src/worlds/night/sky.ts` undoes the grade of the unit on the map per channel (gain, tone curve, saturation and the darkening toward the corners), so the sky on the screen has the tones of the take.

## Not maps
