# Recipe · world frost

## No files

- This world ships no file of its own. Its stand-in backdrop (the approved image with the plant painted out, `backdrop.jpg` in two sizes) was removed with its entry in `tools/standin-backdrops.ts` once the sky was computed; `backdrop.file` is `null` in `src/worlds/frost/index.ts`.

## The sky (no file)

- Decision: the frost sky is not a painting. It is fog and nothing else, a smooth grey with a wide glow round the sun, and an 8 bit picture of so smooth a field bands when the grade stretches it. The golden recipe (paint the approved sky with the plant out) is right for a sky with clouds in it and wrong for this one.
- What draws it: `src/worlds/frost/sky.ts`, a full screen pass that reads `fogLight(direction)` from `src/weather/fog/index.ts`: the fog's color at eye level (`fog.color`), its color overhead (`FOG_HIGH` in `src/worlds/frost/index.ts`), the sun's light through it (`fog.sunTint`) and the glow round the sun (`sky.glow`), plus the white disc (`sky.disc`) and a deck of thicker and thinner fog overhead, about a sixth either way in five sizes, that drifts with the wind (`WISPS`). Its strength was set by the approved image: after a smooth surface is taken out of the sky box (20, 20, 620, 400), blurred 3 px, the approved keeps 0.84 levels rms and ours 0.93. Away from the sun the thinner patches of the deck are a little deeper and bluer on a pattern of their own (`SLATE`, up to 5 parts in 100 of red, 3 of green, 1 of blue, gone inside about 0.3 rad of the sun), so the upper left is a cool slate that varies and not one grey; within about 0.2 rad of the sun the deck's mottling is a fifth stronger (`WISPS_SUN`), which brings the box right of the sun (1560, 20, 1900, 260) from 0.67 to 0.82 levels rms against the approved's 0.83.
- How the numbers were found: a least squares fit of those four colors to the approved image `references/approved/frost.png`, scaled to 1920 x 1080 and taken back through the grade to scene values, over the sky above y 560 with the plant masked out. The far shapes, the banks and the neighbors fade toward the same `fogLight`, so a thing that is gone in the fog is exactly the sky behind it.
- Checks: the tones of named boxes of the sky beside the approved at 1920 x 1080 are in the reports of `agent/frost-world`.

## Decisions (no file)

- Air: the air of the frost is the fog and its drifting sheets (`BANKS` in `src/weather/fog/index.ts`), rings round the plant's axis at 1.9, 2.5, 3, 3.8, 5, 7, 11 and 20 m, which is 2.6 m from the lens for the first, and one more (`FIRST`) at 1.2 m in front of the first row. The even part of the fog is thin (`fog.density` 0.012) because it lies along the ground and would grey the plant itself. The air thickens with distance, and steeply: the sheets in front of the first four rows are thin (0.05 to 0.17), so those rows keep their own darks and their own frost, and the sheets from 5 m on are dense (0.34 to 0.7), so every row behind is paler and the far rows are gone. This was set in the last fix pass against the critics' words (the middle ground read as a flat olive grey): with the earlier, denser near sheets the darkest fifth of the left rows (x 60 to 700, y 650 to 800) stood at 54, 65, 65 against the approved 41, 58, 55; it now stands at the approved's level. The three nearest sheets end at the neighbors' heads (1.55 m). `FIRST` veils the first row's heads, is thin under 0.6 to 1 of the plant's height and is gone by 39 s (`fogFarAt`), so the ridge in the unit's last second reads as it did without it. Half of each sheet is solid and half the drifting banks. Under the leaves, below 0.3 to 0.7 of the plant's height, the sheets are thin (`BANK_UNDER`), because the fog lies over the closed canopy and the lowest rows stay the darkest. Within 0.12 to 0.34 rad of the sun they are not thinned (`SUN_SIDE`): toward the sun the fog glows all the way down, which veils the rows on the sun's side as in the approved image. Down in the canopy the fog gets less of the sky (`DEEP_NEAR`, `DEEP_FAR`, from 0.4 of the plant's height to its top, read from `heightAt`) and is teal, not grey (`BANK_AMONG`); the sun's share of a sheet grows with its depth (`SUN_NEAR` at `NEAR_M` to `SUN_FAR` by `FAR_M`, 1.9 to 4.5 m) and is cooler low down (`SUN_AMONG`), so the side toward the sun is the paler one without turning brown. How the numbers were found: rows of 40 px across two columns of the frame (x 60 to 700 and x 1500 to 1900, y 480 to 1000), 10th, 50th and 90th percentile of luma beside the approved image at 1920 x 1080, and the mean color of the darkest fifth, the median and the brightest fifth of seven boxes over the neighbors. Ice specks in the air were tried and dropped: at the size the lens gives them they read as dust on the glass.
- The sun's halo: `fogLightIn(direction, mist)` in `src/weather/fog/index.ts`. Round the disc the fog takes the blue and some green out of the sun's light in a ring that is strongest about 0.05 rad out (`FOG_RING`, `FOG_RING_TAKES`), deeper under the sun than over it (`FOG_RING_UP`, `FOG_HALO_OVER`), and the peach glow reaches about as far (`FOG_HALO`); beyond it the sun's side is only paler (`FOG_WIDE`, the eighth power of the cosine), which is the cool grey round the halo, and down in the thick fog along the ground the halo is spent (`FOG_HALO_LOW`). The sky passes the drifting deck's own density as `mist`, so the halo reaches farther and is richer where the fog is thick (`FOG_SHAPE`, `FOG_MIST`, `FOG_RING_MIST`) and moves with the deck at the deck's speed; a faint, slow unevenness by bearing round the sun (`SHAFTS` in `src/worlds/frost/sky.ts`) stands for the shafts the uneven fog leaves. Level with the sun and over it the fog keeps a faint peach skirt farther out, shaped by the same mist: a little green and blue gone about 0.135 rad out (`FOG_SKIRT_AT`, `FOG_SKIRT_WIDE`, `FOG_SKIRT_TAKES`) and a little more red beyond that, fading over 0.25 rad (`FOG_SKIRT_FAR`, `FOG_SKIRT_ADDS`); it was set by the ring averages at 190 to 340 px from the sun and the upper middle and upper right boxes of the sky, each within 2 levels of the approved after it. The sheets and the far shapes read the same light with no mist. How the numbers were found: luma and red minus blue along six rays from the sun's centre (up, right, up right, up left, down right, left) at 40 to 430 px, blurred 6 px, beside the approved image.
- Ground: no frame of the unit shows the ground. Draft stills of frames 900 to 1170 every 30 frames and 1199, and the `depth` view of frame 900, show the neighbors' leaves over every pixel under the horizon. So no crystals are placed on the grass and `src/ground/frost/` holds nothing. The pale crust on the soil and the grass is the ground's own frost state (`ground.frost: 1`). Open: if a later camera or a thinner field shows the ground in this unit, crystals from `src/weather/frost/` go on the grass blades.
- Far shapes: the nine layers of the shared skyline, drawn from `skylineRing` so they follow the film's one easing; their top edge is the shared one, so nothing steps at the cut to the golden hour. The far land under them lies at 1.3 m at every time of the unit, as the night's does before the cut and the golden hour's after it (the line that would lower it with the plant's height never reached the mesh; at the key it stands 3.5 px over the horizon behind every sheet, so it was left where both cuts meet), and is seen as faintly as the hills on it (`LAND_SEE` in `src/worlds/frost/layers.ts`), so no line of bare fog lies between the field's far edge and the ridge. A fade of the hills' feet into the fog was tried and dropped: it made that line.
- The far shapes through the unit: on the first frame after the cut from the night they stand as a faint trace in the fog (`SEE_CUT`) and the fog closes over them in 4 s (`fogCloseAt`); they stand faintest through the middle of the unit (`SEE_FAINT`) and come out as the fog thins between 36.5 and 39 s (`fogFarAt`, `SEE_GAIN`), so the last second before the cut shows the ridge. The far sheet stands lower (1.65 m) and the three near ones end at 1.55 m so the ridge is seen over them. Open: on the side away from the sun the ridge at the cut is still about half as strong as the cut was fitted with.
- `standIn` is `false` from this point: the sky, the far layers, the air, the state of the ground and the weather are all drawn by the parts above.
