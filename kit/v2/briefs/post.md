# Brief: the lens

Your folder is `src/post/`. Your branch is `agent/post`, your looks name is `post`.

`src/post/post.ts` is the last step of every frame: it fills the out of focus blur (`fill.ts`), scatters light in the lens (bloom, veil, halation), adds what a low sun does to a lens (`glare.ts`: glow, star, shafts, spokes), grades and adds grain (`grade.ts`). `film.ts` calls `post.render(renderer, frame, def, frameIndex, samples, diag, sun)` once a frame: keep that call. The numbers of the grade (`def.grade`: exposure, contrast, white point, saturation, gain, lift, bloom, vignette, grain) are the Builder's, set for each instant in `src/v2/look.ts`. The lens is yours.

## What changes

1. One lens for the whole film. `src/post/looks.ts` holds a table of lens looks by version 1 scene (`dawn`, `rain`, `night`, `frost`, `golden`, and a blend toward the field by scene time). Version 2 has one place and no scenes, and today every frame uses the nearly empty entry `place`. Make the lens look a pure function of the instant: `now` and `later` from `src/v2/now.ts`, the weights of the kinds of light from `weightsAt` in `src/v2/look.ts` (`day`, `warm`, `storm`, `frost`, `night`, which sum to 1), where the sun sits in the frame (`sun`: `x`, `y`, `weight`) and how much of it reaches the lens (`sunRead` already measures what shows of it). No value may jump: the sun crosses the frame's edge twice a day in the time lapse, 30 times in the film, and comes out from behind leaves, the stem and clouds all the time. Read "What version 1 left behind" in `COMMON.md`.
2. The sun in the lens. It rises and sets in front of the camera all film long: this is the signature of the film and it has to look like a cinema lens pointed at a real sun, every time, at every height. A small clean core, a soft glow that takes the colour of the air, a veil of flare that lowers contrast over the whole frame when the sun is in it and lifts as it leaves, a wrap of light round the edges of whatever stands before it, and at low sun in haze the fan of light version 1's critic missed in the bloom: "a has a broad sunburst: long god-rays fanning up into the sky and down across the hills, with a hazy glow wrapping the leaf edge. b has only a small hot spot". No coloured ghosts in a row, no star with hard spikes, no bokeh orbs or floating specks (a critic counted those against version 1: "full of large round bokeh orbs and floating specks").
3. Shafts. Twice the film needs light standing in the air: at bar 41 (69.5 s, `references/v2/keys/break.png`) the sun tears through the storm deck and rays fan down from the gap through the last rain (`dials.burst`), and in the frost hold from bar 23 (38.6 s) the sun clears the mist. Rays come from what blocks the sun: cast them from the sun's place in the frame through the picture's own bright and dark, so clouds and leaves cut them, strong where the air is thick (`fog`, `rain`, `burst`) and gone in clear air.
4. The flash. `dials.flash` at bars 33 and 37: the lens blooms for a few frames where the cloud lights up. It must not flatten the frame to white.
5. One sample. Live, the film is drawn with one sample a frame, and `fill.ts` then makes the whole depth of field blur from the blur circle each pixel carries in alpha. That is what Brandon will record, so it is the look that counts most: edges of sharp things in front of blurred ones, blurred things in front of sharp ones, the blurred sun, thin bright lines like wet grass and rain. Compare 1 sample with 48 on shots 02, 07, 10 and 14 and close the gap where you can.
6. Cost. Count your passes over the screen at 1920 x 1080 and their sizes, and say so in your report. `view.quality` is not passed to you: tell `/root` how you want it (a setter is the obvious way) and make `low` skip or shrink what costs most. Everything you need is made in the constructor and `setSize`; nothing after.
7. The grade's shape (`grade.ts`: the curve, the knee, how highlights lose their hue, the blacks) stays close to version 1's `golden` and `dawn` looks, which were approved. Film grain stays fine and even. No vignette that reads as one.

## What is judged

2. Clips with `--only all --view film`: `--clip 5.5,8.5` (a sunset and a night: the sun leaves the frame), `--clip 68.5,72` (the break), `--clip 96,100` (the bloom's sun). The stand-in sky has no clouds: judge the shafts with something in front of the sun, and again when `/root` tells you the real sky is in.
