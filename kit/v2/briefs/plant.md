# Brief: the plant

Your folders are `src/plant/`, without `src/plant/surface/wet/`, `src/plant/surface/frost/` and `src/plant/surface/dew/` (the rain and frost agents have those), and the one file `src/v2/inputs.ts`. Your branch is `agent/plant`, your looks name is `plant`.

The sunflower of version 1 is the best thing in it and it stays: its shapes, its skin, its growth line (`config.plant`, `src/plant/growth.ts`). `film.ts` calls `plant.prepare(tau, tauClose, inputs, inputsClose)` once a frame and that call stays. Your work is how the plant lives through fifteen days and two holds, and the defects version 1's critics named.

## Brandon's words

"The issue I have is that the flower essentially keeps growing throughout the whole film. That's not exactly what I wanted for the rainy, frosty scenes the flower should not be growing ... we get to just take in the flower with the rain hitting it or the flower with the frost on it and then that will go away and then we have another grow cycle."

## What changes

1. Growth runs on `tau` and nothing else. In a hold `tau` stands still (12 in the frost, 28.3 in the storm), so nothing that grows may read any other clock. Read "What version 1 left behind" in `COMMON.md`: `src/plant/growth.ts`, `src/plant/leaves/index.ts`, `src/plant/stem/index.ts`, `src/plant/surface/index.ts` and others read version 1's scene times (a posture for the rain scene, a droop for the night scene, light that begins at the field scene). Each becomes either a function of `tau` or of what the plant feels, which arrives in `PlantInputs`. You own `PlantInputs` and `src/v2/inputs.ts`, where a `Moment` becomes what the plant feels: extend both as you need (the cold, the rain, the night, the hour), keep every field the field agent and `film.ts` already read (`leanX`, `leanZ`, `lift`, `wind`, `calm`), and keep the exported names of `src/plant/growth.ts`, `src/plant/model.ts`, `src/plant/part.ts` and `src/plant/index.ts`, because `src/field/` and `src/ground/` import them. If one has to change, tell `/root` first.
2. The days. A young sunflower follows the sun from east to west and swings back before dawn; its leaves lift in the light and hang in the dark. With a day passing in 3.4 to 6.9 seconds this is the motion of the time lapse and it must read as alive: the tip swinging after the sun with a little lag, leaves rising and sinking in a wave from the top down, never a metronome and never a jerk at the turn of the night. The sun's path is in `Moment` (`sun`, `sunAzimuthDeg`, `sunElevationDeg`, `phase`); a lag is the sun of an earlier second, since no state may be carried between frames. The mature head stops following and faces the lens (east), as real ones do.
3. The time lapse and real time. In the time lapse (`dials.lapse` near 1) leaves in real footage are restless: small quick settles and twitches frame to frame as growth and wind are sampled. In the holds and in the bloom (`lapse` 0.02) it is real time: slow sway, weight, follow through. Make both, driven by `lapse`, seeded by position and time, and look at clips, not stills, to judge them.
4. The frost hold (bars 17 to 25, `tau` 12, about 19 cm tall, three pairs of leaves, seen from 58 cm at 48 mm, `references/v2/keys/frost.png`). The plant stands still in cold air: leaves a little cupped and lowered, stiff, a faint tremble at most. When the thaw comes (bar 23 to 25, `frost` falling, `sunThrough` rising) the leaves ease up again.
5. The storm hold (bars 33 to 41, `tau` 28.3, about 90 cm, seen whole from 2.4 m at 38 mm, `references/v2/keys/storm.png`). Wind in gusts (`dials.wind` 0.54) bends the stem and throws the leaves, and each leaf nods when a drop hits it and springs back: many small nods at different times, on `uReal`. The rain agent draws the water on the skin; you move the leaves.
6. The first two seconds (`references/approved/dawn-sprout.png`). Critics: "the hook does not push up out of the soil; it grows as a whole from a small arch into a big one", "the hooked sprout has a flat-shaded, bald matte grey-green side face bounded by a hard white rim crease, a grid-like row of hair dots". The arch must come up through the soil, straining, soil on its back, and straighten. The emergence numbers shared with the soil are in `src/ground/soil.ts` (the ground agent's file): read them, and tell `/root` if they need to change.
7. The bloom and the crane (bars 57 to 73). The opening is version 1's best passage. One critic on its last eight seconds: "The camera retreat supplies most of the visible change. The open flower retains essentially the same petal arrangement, and the neighboring head remains closed." From bar 61 to 73 `tau` runs from 51 to 60: the head should go on living (rays settling, the disc's rings of florets opening inward, the head growing heavier).
8. Cost. `plant.prepare` builds geometry on the processor every frame, and the film has to hold 30 frames a second live. Measure what one call costs (`performance.now` in a test harness is fine, it changes no pixel) for the hero at tau 12, 28.3 and 51, and say so in your report. If a call costs more than 3 ms on this machine, find out why.

## What is judged

2. Clips with `--only sky,ground,plant --view film`: `--clip 0,3` (the hook), `--clip 43.5,50.5` (two days: the swing and the leaves), `--clip 57,62` (rain and gusts in real time; the rain itself is another agent's), `--clip 96,104` (the bloom).
3. No growth in a hold: draw the plant alone in flat light at the first and the last frame of each hold with the wind at 0 and show that the two are the same picture (`film.diag = 'plant'` is the flat light; tell `/root` what switch you need for the wind).
4. `--count` prints PASS, and the line of `COMMON.md` prints nothing for your folders.

A first heartbeat with pictures within 30 minutes. Your report goes to `/root` 150 minutes after you start, or sooner if you are done.
