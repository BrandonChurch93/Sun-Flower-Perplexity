Subject: the edge of one true leaf of a young sunflower seedling, seen from the side and a little from below, very close: the frame is about six centimeters wide in the plane of focus. The leaf comes in from the lower left corner and its pointed tip ends a little right of center, in the upper half of the frame, so its toothed edge runs as one long diagonal across the picture. The leaf is rough and matte, dull grey-green, with shallow uneven teeth along the edge, a pale midrib and raised veins, and short stiff hairs. Behind it, far out of focus, are the soft shapes of the seedling's stem and one more leaf.

Frost: thick white hoarfrost, and it is the subject of the shot. Along the whole edge of the leaf stands a dense fringe of ice crystals: flat feathery blades and fine needles two to four millimeters long, crowded side by side, each one a real crystal with facets, sharp edges and a ribbed, feathered surface, clear at its base and white at its tip. They stand out from the edge at right angles like the teeth of a comb, longest on the points of the leaf teeth and at the tip of the leaf. The upper face of the leaf is sugared with smaller crystals: tiny plates and grains that stand apart from each other with the grey-green of the leaf between them, thickest along the midrib and the veins and thinning in the open fields between the veins. Every stiff hair on the leaf carries its own sleeve of ice. Nothing has melted: there is no water, no drop, no wet sheen.

Light: before the sun has broken through. The sun is a large, dim, pale disc in thick mist behind the leaf, far out of focus in the upper left of the frame, soft enough to look at, with no rays. It backlights the crystals: each blade of ice along the edge is lit through from behind and glows a cold white with a faint pearl core, and a few facets that happen to face the lens carry a small hard white point of light. The face of the leaf toward the camera is in cool blue-grey shade, and its frost is still plainly white in that shade. The light is cold and quiet: pearl grey and grey-blue, with only a trace of peach in the mist round the sun.

A band of the fringed edge about two centimeters long, in the middle of the frame, is perfectly sharp, so that single crystals can be counted; the edge falls softly out of focus toward the lower left corner and toward the tip, and the out-of-focus crystals there turn into small soft round discs of light.
